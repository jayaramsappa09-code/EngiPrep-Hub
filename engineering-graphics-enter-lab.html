Unit I — Introduction, Scales & Curves
Engineering drawing fundamentals, scales, and 7 engineering curves with animated constructions

Principles of Engineering Drawing

Engineering Drawing is the universal language of engineers — using standardized symbols, lines, and conventions to represent physical objects with complete information about shape, size, and manufacturing details.

📐 Why Learn Engineering Graphics?

  • Communicate technical ideas clearly between engineers and manufacturers
  • Represent 3D objects in 2D planes accurately
  • Foundation for CAD tools like AutoCAD, SolidWorks, CATIA
  • Essential for JNTUK examinations and professional practice

BIS Line Types (IS 10714)

Line TypeDescriptionThicknessUsed For
Continuous thickSolid unbroken thick line0.5–0.7mmVisible edges, outlines
Continuous thinSolid unbroken thin line0.25–0.35mmDimension lines, hatching
Dashed thickShort dashes, thick0.5mmHidden edges
Chain thinLong dash, short dash0.25mmCentre lines, axes
Free hand thinIrregular wavy line0.25mmBreak lines
Always use correct line types. Visible edges = thick, hidden edges = dashed, centre lines = chain thin. Wrong line types = marks deducted.

Viva Questions

What BIS standard is followed for line types? ▼
IS 10714 (BIS Standard) specifies visible lines as continuous thick (0.5–0.7mm), hidden lines as dashed thick, centre lines as chain thin, and dimension lines as continuous thin.
What is the standard sheet size used in JNTUK lab? ▼
A1 size (594mm × 841mm) is used in JNTUK Engineering Graphics lab. Sheet sizes follow ISO 216 standard.
What is the difference between First Angle and Third Angle projection? ▼
First Angle (India/Europe): Object between observer and plane. TV below FV on sheet. Third Angle (USA): Plane between observer and object. TV above FV. JNTUK uses First Angle per BIS standard.

Representative Fraction & Scale Types

RF = Drawing Length / Actual Length
Plain Scale
2 units. E.g., meters + decimeters. Uses simple division.
Diagonal Scale
3 units. Uses principle of similar triangles for third unit.
Vernier Scale
3 units, very precise. n VSD = (n-1) MSD principle.

Ellipse — Theory

An ellipse is the locus of a point where the sum of distances from two fixed foci is constant and equal to the major axis.

PF₁ + PF₂ = 2a = Major Axis | Eccentricity: 0 < e < 1

Viva & PYQs

How do you locate foci when major and minor axes are given? ▼
With centre O, radius = semi-major axis (a), draw arc cutting the minor axis. Two intersections are F₁ and F₂. Alternatively: c = √(a²−b²), foci at ±c from centre.
Name the methods of constructing an ellipse. ▼
1. Concentric Circle Method 2. Oblong (Rectangle) Method 3. Arc of Circle (Foci) Method 4. Trammel Method 5. Directrix-Focus Method. For JNTUK: Concentric Circle and Arc methods are most important.

Hyperbola — Theory

Hyperbola: |PF₁ − PF₂| = constant = 2a. Has two branches, two foci, asymptotes.

|PF₁ − PF₂| = 2a | Eccentricity e > 1

Key Points

  • Asymptotes: Lines the curve approaches but never crosses
  • Two branches: Unlike ellipse/parabola
  • Rectangular hyperbola: xy = c², used for Boyle's law curves

Viva Questions

What is a rectangular hyperbola? ▼
A rectangular hyperbola has a = b, angle between asymptotes = 90°. Equation: xy = c². Used to draw Boyle's law (PV = constant) curves in engineering.

Cycloid vs Epicycloid vs Hypocycloid? ▼
Cycloid: rolls on straight line. Epicycloid: rolls outside a circle. Hypocycloid: rolls inside a circle. All trace a point on the circumference of the rolling circle.

Involute of a Circle — Animated

An involute is traced by the end of a taut string unwound from a circle. Used in gear tooth profiles — most gear teeth have involute profiles for smooth, constant-velocity transmission.

Why are involute profiles used in gear teeth? ▼
Involute teeth: 1) Constant speed ratio regardless of small centre distance changes 2) Common normal at contact passes through pitch point 3) Easier to manufacture precisely 4) Wider base = stronger tooth.

Viva & PYQs

What is First Angle Projection and why is it used in India? ▼
First Angle (European): Object in front of VP, above HP. Projections obtained behind the object. India follows BIS using First Angle per ISO standard. Symbol: cone pointing left.
What are traces of a line? ▼
HT: where line meets HP (FV of HT on XY, extend FV to XY then drop perp). VT: where line meets VP (TV of VT on XY, extend TV to XY then raise perp).
What is apparent length? ▼
Projected length of a line as it appears in a view — less than true length when line is inclined to that plane. True length only seen when line is parallel to the projection plane.

Unit III — Projections of Planes
Triangle, square, rectangle, pentagon, hexagon, circle — in all orientations

Key Principles

📐 When does a plane show True Shape?

  • Plane parallel to HP: TV = TRUE SHAPE, FV = horizontal line
  • Plane parallel to VP: FV = TRUE SHAPE, TV = horizontal line
  • Plane inclined to HP at α: FV = line at α to XY, TV = foreshortened
  • Plane inclined to VP at θ: TV = line at θ to XY, FV = foreshortened
  • Plane perpendicular to HP: TV = straight line (edge view)

Viva & PYQs

When does a plane appear as a line in projection? ▼
A plane shows its edge view (line) when it is perpendicular to the projection plane. If perpendicular to HP → TV is a line. If perpendicular to VP → FV is a line.
What is the trace of a plane? ▼
HT = intersection of plane with HP; VT = intersection of plane with VP. Traces define the plane's orientation in space.

Viva & PYQs

Prism vs Pyramid?
Prism: Two identical parallel polygonal bases + rectangular lateral faces. Pyramid: One polygonal base + triangular faces meeting at apex.
FV and TV of cone with axis ⊥ HP?
TV = circle (base) with centre mark (apex). FV = isosceles triangle with base = diameter, height = cone height.

Viva & PYQs

Isometric View vs Isometric Projection? ▼
Isometric View: uses actual dimensions (acceptable in most JNTUK questions). Isometric Projection: uses scaled dimensions (×0.816), slightly smaller. Unless specified, use isometric view.
Why is development of surfaces needed? ▼
Used in: sheet metal fabrication (ducts, pipes), packaging design (boxes), aerospace (hull panels), calculating surface area for painting/coating.

Viva Questions

What is the difference between TRIM and EXTEND? ▼
TRIM removes the portion of a line/arc beyond a cutting edge. EXTEND lengthens a line/arc to meet a boundary. Both require selecting cutting/boundary edges first, then selecting the entity to trim/extend.
What is the OFFSET command used for? ▼
OFFSET creates a parallel copy of a line, arc, circle, or polyline at a specified distance. Used to draw parallel lines, concentric circles, and uniform spacing in drawings.
Difference between ARRAY Rectangular and Polar? ▼
Rectangular Array: Copies objects in rows and columns at specified row/column spacing. Polar Array: Copies objects in a circular pattern at equal angular spacing around a centre point. E.g., holes on a bolt circle use Polar Array.
What is the use of FILLET command? ▼
FILLET creates a smooth circular arc between two lines at a specified radius. At radius=0, it creates a sharp corner. Used for rounding corners of mechanical parts, creating tangent arcs between edges.
What is a LAYER in AutoCAD and why is it used? ▼
Layers are like transparent overlays in AutoCAD. Objects on different layers can be shown/hidden, frozen, locked, or printed differently. In engineering drawings: separate layers for visible lines, hidden lines, centre lines, dimensions, text, etc. Follows BIS convention where each line type is on a different layer with appropriate color and lineweight.

Quick Revision Cards

📐 Unit I — Curves

  • Ellipse: e < 1, PF₁+PF₂ = 2a
  • Parabola: e = 1, PF = PM
  • Hyperbola: e > 1, |PF₁-PF₂| = 2a
  • Cycloid: rolls on straight line
  • Epicycloid: rolls outside circle
  • Hypocycloid: rolls inside circle
  • Involute: unwinding string, gear teeth

📐 Unit II — Points & Lines

  • Q-I: above HP, front VP → FV↑, TV↓
  • Q-II: above HP, behind VP → both↑
  • Q-III: below HP, behind VP → FV↓, TV↑
  • Q-IV: below HP, front VP → both↓
  • ∥ both planes → TL in both FV & TV
  • HT: line meets HP; VT: line meets VP

📐 Unit III — Planes

  • ∥ HP → TV = true shape, FV = line
  • ∥ VP → FV = true shape, TV = line
  • ⊥ HP → TV = edge (line view)
  • ⊥ VP → FV = edge (line view)
  • HT = plane ∩ HP; VT = plane ∩ VP

📐 Unit IV — Solids

  • Axis ⊥ HP → TV = base, FV = face
  • Axis ⊥ VP → FV = base, TV = face
  • Two-step for inclined axis
  • Cylinder FV = rect, TV = circle
  • Cone FV = triangle, TV = circle
  • Cube: 6F, 12E, 8V (Euler: F+V-E=2)

📐 Unit V — Isometric & Dev

  • Iso scale = 0.816 (cos30°/cos45°)
  • Iso axes: 120° apart, 30° to horizontal
  • Cylinder dev: W=πD rectangle
  • Cone dev: θ = (r/l)×360°, r_sector = l
  • l (slant) = √(r² + h²)

📐 AutoCAD Key Commands

  • L = LINE, C = CIRCLE, REC = RECTANGLE
  • TR = TRIM, EX = EXTEND
  • MI = MIRROR, AR = ARRAY
  • F = FILLET, CHA = CHAMFER
  • O = OFFSET, DIM = DIMENSION
  • Z = ZOOM, H = HATCH

Exam Tips & Common Mistakes

Always draw Reference Line (XY) first with proper labelling. Both HP and VP must be clearly indicated.
Use correct line types: Visible = thick continuous, Hidden = thick dashed, Centre = chain thin, Dimension = thin continuous.
Label all points with proper notation: FV points = a'b'c', TV points = abc, actual points = A,B,C.
Projectors must be perpendicular to XY in orthographic projection. Never draw projectors at an angle.
MISTAKE: Forgetting to show hidden edges in dashed lines. All edges behind the visible surface must be shown as dashed.
MISTAKE: Drawing cycloid base line shorter than 2πR. Always calculate: base = πd (full circumference).
MISTAKE: Using actual scale instead of isometric scale when projection is asked. Iso scale = 0.816.
MISTAKE: Not writing RF value below scale drawing. Always write RF = 1/n and scale statement.
